Our Story

Two brothers, one Youniverse.

Mahesh Nishar and Amit Nishar didn't start with a factory — they started with a single retail clothing shop in Kalyan. It did well enough to become three, spread across Thane and Kalyan, each one run the same hands-on way.

In 2015, the brothers took the leap from selling clothes to making them — a small manufacturing unit in Vikhroli, and the first stitches of what would become Youniverse.

"We started small, kept learning, and kept making. The space is bigger now, but the hands-on way we work remains the same." Mahesh & Amit Nishar, Founders

From Diwali 2025, Youniverse moved production into a new, larger space in Dadar West — the next chapter for a family business built on fabric, fit and original expression.

The Journal

Notes on how the clothes actually get made, from the Dadar workshop.

Craft

Why We Still Hand-Paint Every Shirt

No two Youniverse shirts are ever quite the same — and that's on purpose.

Read the story
Every Youniverse print starts as a brush, not a machine. Before a shirt is cut, the fabric is laid flat across a workbench in our Dadar workshop and painted panel by panel — a butterfly here, a Warli dancer there, a line-drawn face on a sleeve. It's slower than screen printing, and it means every batch has small, honest variations. We've kept it this way since 2015 because it's the whole point of the label. A machine print looks the same on the thousandth shirt as it did on the first. A hand-painted one doesn't — and that's what makes it feel like it belongs to the person wearing it. So if your Youniverse shirt looks a little different from the one in the shop photo, that's not a defect. That's the brushstroke.

Process

Wide-Leg, Zero Off-Cuts

Our patchwork denim isn't a trend print — it's what's left over from everything else we cut.

Read the story
Every Youniverse patchwork jean starts as offcuts — leftover denim sorted by wash and weight, then pieced back together into a single wide leg before it's painted or embroidered. It's more work than cutting a pair from a single bolt — every panel has to be matched and seamed by hand — but very little denim leaves our workshop as waste. The Warli panels and portrait prints you see on our jeans aren't hiding the patchwork; they're painted straight onto it. We'd rather piece it together than throw it out.

Future

From Dadar to the World

Pan-India was the first chapter. Mahesh and Amit Nishar on what comes after.

Read the story
Youniverse grew from a single Kalyan shop into a pan-India label because retailers kept finding the work — first three shops, then a manufacturing unit in Vikhroli in 2015, and now a bigger home in Dadar since Diwali 2025. "Every market we've entered so far has been because a retailer found us, not the other way around," says Amit. "The next step is doing that on purpose — outside India." Mahesh adds that the plan isn't to change what Youniverse is to get there: the hand-painting, the in-house cutting, the custom orders — that's the part that travels best. For now, that means growing the wholesale side and slowly introducing the label to retailers beyond India, while keeping every piece painted and stitched in the same workshop it always has been.
